## Conclusions, bounded

- Generic Mars map, route optimization, hazards and points of interest are **not differentiators**: already proposed in several public profiles.
- Team Delta Dune's technical baseline might be substantial, but claims of simulator and papers have not been independently verified.
- Absence of a feature from a public description does not mean a team lacks it.
- The user's approx. 671 registered teams and hypothetical 1000 are **planning figures**, not a verified total for this challenge. No meaningful win probability can be inferred from them.

## Hypothesized defensible differentiators (not completed)

1. Reproducible, verifiably georeferenced multi-source data layers, with documented resampling and registration error.
2. Explicit measured/derived/assumed data separation.
3. Transparent, uncertainty-aware terrain and route cost modeling.
4. Distance-only baseline versus hypothetical slope-aware route comparisons, and a sensitivity analysis.
5. Coverage-aware refusal to recommend a route when measurements are missing or unsuitable.
6. Science destinations backed by archival source/provenance and cautious interpretation.
7. Coherent, quick-to-understand mission scenario and accessible demo.

## Five research questions

- Q1: What is the physically meaningful smallest spatial scale of a candidate terrain model, and how do we detect false resolution?
- Q2: Can independently sourced Mars observations be co-registered accurately enough for the intended decision?
- Q3: How can we state terrain difficulty without inventing astronaut traverse safety thresholds?
- Q4: Under what disturbances do route rankings reverse? What uncertainty is decision-critical?
- Q5: Can a judge follow the evidence supporting each layer and output in under two minutes?
